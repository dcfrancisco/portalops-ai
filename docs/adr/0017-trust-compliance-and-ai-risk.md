# ADR-0017: Trust, compliance and AI risk management

Status: Proposed

## Context

PortalOps handles personal data, can change production systems, processes voice and video, and uses AI models. This puts it in scope of privacy law (GDPR and equivalents), AI regulation (such as the EU AI Act), security frameworks (such as ISO 27001 and SOC 2) and sector rules. Controls so far are scattered across ADRs.

## Decision

- **Data governance:** a data map per store and flow (portal data, memory, artifacts, telemetry, audit). Lawful basis and purpose per flow. Retention schedules with automatic deletion. **Right to erasure** is implemented across memory, embeddings, artifacts and episodic records (delete by subject and by source document, with vector removal). Audit records are pseudonymized so erasure does not break integrity. Data residency is configurable per tenant, including provider region selection.
- **AI risk management:** an AI system register and model cards per provider and model (purpose, data handling, evaluation results from ADR-0013, known limits). A risk assessment and DPIA/FRIA template ships with the product. Transparency: users are told when they interact with AI, AI-generated findings and spoken output are labeled, and each recommendation links to its evidence and decision trail. Human oversight is built in (approvals, kill switches, override). Biometric identification is excluded (ADR-0010). Emotion inference is excluded.
- **Provider governance:** zero-retention and no-training terms are required for external providers, with a subprocessor list. Local or private-endpoint models are supported for restricted tenants (ADR-0002).
- **Security program:** threat model maintained with the design (STRIDE and the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications), secure SDLC, dependency scanning, SBOM generation, signed releases and signed edge artifacts, penetration tests before major releases, vulnerability disclosure policy.
- **Incident response:** playbooks for AI-specific incidents (harmful action, data leak through a model, injection exploit, physical misuse), including kill-switch use, forensic export from the run store and audit log, and notification obligations.
- **Compliance evidence:** automated evidence packs (audit extracts, decision-table versions, approval records, access reviews) generated by the Audit domain for SOC 2, ISO 27001 and regulator requests.
- **Accessibility and inclusion:** WCAG 2.2 AA for the workspace, voice and visual alternatives for every interaction, and multilingual support (Liferay locales) for prompts, speech and outputs.

## Consequences

- Positive: a credible enterprise and regulated-sector story, reduced legal and incident risk.
- Negative: ongoing governance work, cross-store erasure complexity, and added documentation burden.
- Negative: regulation is evolving, so requirements must be re-reviewed each release.
